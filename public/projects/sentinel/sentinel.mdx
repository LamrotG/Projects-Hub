# Overview

Sentinel is a cybercrime investigation and intelligence platform designed for investigators, analysts, and digital forensic teams.

The platform consolidates investigation workflows into a unified environment where cases, evidence, entities, timelines, and investigative reasoning are managed together. Rather than treating investigations as isolated records, Sentinel emphasizes the relationships between digital artifacts and the analytical process behind cybercrime investigations.

Its primary objective is to support structured investigations while providing investigators with tools for visual reasoning and collaborative analysis.

---

# Background

Cybercrime investigations often require investigators to work across multiple disconnected tools including spreadsheets, documents, evidence repositories, communication platforms, and reporting software.

As investigations grow in complexity, maintaining context between evidence, people, infrastructure, and events becomes increasingly difficult.

Sentinel was conceived as a platform that centralizes these workflows while supporting the way investigators naturally think—through relationships, timelines, and evolving investigative theories.

---

# Problem

Traditional investigation workflows commonly suffer from:

* fragmented evidence storage
* disconnected investigative notes
* poor visualization of relationships
* manual timeline reconstruction
* limited collaboration
* inconsistent access control

These limitations make complex investigations harder to manage and more difficult to communicate.

---

# Objectives

The project was designed around several objectives:

* Centralize cybercrime investigations.
* Organize evidence in structured case files.
* Model relationships between investigative entities.
* Support collaborative investigations through role-based access.
* Provide visual tools for analytical reasoning.
* Generate structured reports for investigation outcomes.

---

# Solution

Sentinel provides investigators with a centralized intelligence platform where every investigation is represented as an individual case.

Each case combines:

* evidence
* entities
* timelines
* investigative notes
* visual relationship mapping
* reporting

Rather than forcing investigators into rigid workflows, Sentinel enables investigations to evolve naturally while preserving structure and traceability.

---

# Key Features

## Case Management

Investigations are organized as independent cases with controlled access.

Capabilities include:

* case creation
* investigator assignment
* collaboration management
* lead investigator ownership
* secure case permissions

---

## Evidence Management

Evidence serves as the foundation of every investigation.

Investigators can:

* upload evidence
* organize digital artifacts
* update evidence records
* associate evidence with cases
* establish relationships between evidence and investigative entities

---

## Entity Intelligence

The platform models investigation entities including:

* people
* organizations
* IP addresses
* domains
* devices
* cryptocurrency wallets

Relationships between entities allow investigators to identify connections that would otherwise remain hidden.

---

## Investigation Workspace

The investigation workspace is the core feature of Sentinel.

Instead of relying solely on tables and dashboards, investigators work within an interactive visual environment.

Objects can be positioned, connected, and analyzed to construct investigative theories and reveal patterns across evidence.

This workspace reflects the reasoning process commonly used during complex investigations.

---

## Timeline Reconstruction

Investigators can reconstruct incidents chronologically by linking evidence and events into a coherent investigative timeline.

This improves understanding of attack progression and supports report generation.

---

## Reporting

Sentinel provides structured reporting capabilities that summarize investigations into organized documents suitable for presentation and review.

---

## Notifications

Real-time notifications keep collaborators informed of invitations, investigation activity, and case updates while respecting access permissions.

---

# Architecture

The platform follows a modular architecture that separates presentation, business logic, and data management.

```text
Presentation Layer

↓

Case Management

Evidence System

Entity Intelligence

Workspace

Timeline

Reporting

↓

Database
```

This separation allows features to evolve independently while maintaining a consistent investigation model.

---

# Design Philosophy

Sentinel intentionally avoids the visual style of generic administrative dashboards.

The interface emphasizes:

* information density
* clear hierarchy
* focused workflows
* minimal visual noise
* professional investigative tooling

Every design decision supports investigator productivity rather than decorative interfaces.

# Engineering Challenges

Designing Sentinel required balancing structured case management with flexible investigative workflows.

Some notable challenges included:

* representing complex entity relationships
* organizing heterogeneous evidence
* supporting visual reasoning alongside traditional data views
* designing scalable permission models
* maintaining clarity within data-dense interfaces

---

# Lessons Learned

Working on Sentinel reinforced several software engineering principles.

* Complex workflows benefit from clear information architecture.
* Visualization can significantly improve investigative reasoning.
* Strong access control should be designed early rather than added later.
* Domain-driven design leads to cleaner feature boundaries.
* Modular systems are easier to extend as requirements evolve.
